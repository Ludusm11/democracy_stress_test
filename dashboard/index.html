        What this reading can claimIf a shock like this hit France, past European episodes suggest changes within these ranges.
        What it cannot claimThat the shock will happen, when, or how likely it is. Effects are associations from past episodes, not proven causes.

        For Mark: plugging in real results

        All numbers come from data.js: baseline values in outcomes, effects in effects (one row per scenario × parameter × outcome × horizon: effect_per_unit, ci_low, ci_high, n_events; horizon 1–4 = waves after, 0 = next election). Reading = baseline + Σ effect_per_unit × slider value. Rule: effects used for France are estimated without France. backtest holds the leave-one-out results (each episode predicted by a model estimated without it); france_check compares the model's range with what happened in France. To test effects quickly, load a CSV with the same columns as effects_template.csv. Scenario E does not use these rows: its effects are not additive across parameters (a base effect plus two slopes whose estimates are correlated, and a second dose-response shape), so adding up separate ranges would overstate the uncertainty. It reads grid instead, the reading and its joint bootstrap range on a grid of energy rise × duration, from outputs/model/e_grid.csv, and interpolates between grid points.
